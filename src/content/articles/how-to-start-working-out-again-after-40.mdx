If it has been five, ten, or twenty years since exercise felt normal, you are not behind — you are simply
starting from where you are. That is the only honest starting point anyone has.

## Start smaller than feels impressive

The most common mistake after 40 is restarting at the intensity you remember from your twenties. Muscles,
tendons, and joints adapt at different speeds, and the fastest way to quit again is to get hurt or exhausted in
week one. Two or three manageable sessions a week, done consistently, beat any heroic seven-day plan.

## What actually matters in the first month

- **Showing up** — consistency is the entire goal. Intensity comes later.
- **Learning movements** — squat, hinge, push, pull, and carry patterns at a level your body accepts today.
- **A schedule you can keep** — pick times you can protect on a busy week, not ideal times.
- **Sleep and food that support recovery** — nothing extreme, just enough structure to feel the difference.

## When a coach makes the difference

If you are unsure what the equipment does, worried about form, or you simply know from experience that
accountability is what keeps you going, one-on-one coaching removes the guesswork. A good coach starts with an
honest assessment of your current ability and builds progressions from there — no shame, no punishment
workouts.
